Best Aroid Soil Mix Recipes for Monstera, Philodendron & Anthurium

A good aroid soil mix should hold enough moisture for the roots to use while still draining freely and leaving plenty of air spaces. For many Monsteras and Philodendrons, a practical starting recipe is 3 parts orchid bark, 2 parts coco coir or peat moss, 2 parts perlite or pumice, 1 part horticultural charcoal, and 1 part worm castings. Different aroids need different moisture levels, so this guide compares a general chunky mix with a more moisture-retentive Anthurium and Alocasia blend. Why Your Variegated Syngonium Is Losing Its Color

When a variegated syngonium suddenly begins producing plain, solid green leaves, the cause is typically a shift in environmental conditions or a natural genetic response to stress. Variegated plants contain less chlorophyll than their solid green counterparts, which means they must work harder to photosynthesize. If the growing environment does not support this extra effort, or if the plant undergoes a genetic shift, the vibrant cream, white, or yellow patches will begin to fade or disappear entirely in new growth. Philodendron Root Rot: Signs, Treatment & Rescue Steps

Philodendron root rot is easiest to save when you confirm it early instead of treating every yellow leaf as an overwatering problem. Quick answer: if a Philodendron is wilting while the potting mix is still wet, unpot it and inspect the roots. Firm roots are healthy; soft, dark, slimy, hollow or foul-smelling roots strongly indicate rot. Is Sphagnum Moss Actually the Holy Grail for Rare Plant Propagation?

Long-fiber sphagnum moss is popular for rooting Monstera, Philodendron, and other aroid cuttings because it can hold moisture while leaving space for air. It is not a foolproof medium: when saturated, compressed, or sealed without ventilation, it can keep a node too wet and contribute to rot. The result depends more on preparation, container design, temperature, and monitoring than on the moss itself. The Most Common Failure: Airless Moss Packing soaked moss firmly into a container removes the air spaces that new roots need. A buried node can remain continuously wet, while a closed vessel slows evaporation and makes it harder to notice excess moisture. ...

How Much Light Do Rare Aroids Need? Indoor Light Guide

How much light does a rare aroid actually need? The phrase “bright, indirect light” is useful as a warning against harsh sun, but it is too vague to tell you where to place a Monstera, Philodendron, Anthurium or Alocasia. Quick answer: start with moderate, diffused light, observe the newest growth, and increase exposure gradually. Many established aroids perform well around 250-500 foot-candles (FC), while some acclimated variegated plants can use brighter diffused conditions.
