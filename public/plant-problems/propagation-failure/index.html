Why Is My Monstera Cutting Not Growing? Check the Node First

If a Monstera or rare aroid cutting will not grow, check for a viable node, axillary bud, root health, moisture and stable light before changing media.

Use the Symptom Checker Browse all plant problems
Quick answer

What this symptom can mean

A cutting can root without producing a new vine if the plant material does not include a viable growth point. Start with the node and axillary bud, then check root condition and the stability of the rooting environment.

Check first

Look for these clues

  1. Confirm the cutting includes a real stem node rather than only a leaf and petiole.
  2. Look for a visible or plausible axillary bud at the node.
  3. Inspect roots for healthy growth versus soft or failing tissue.
  4. Check whether the rooting medium is staying airy rather than saturated.
Possible causes

Do not assume one cause

  • No viable growth pointA leaf-only cutting can remain alive or form limited roots but cannot create a new climbing vine without the correct stem tissue.
  • Root-zone stressA saturated, compacted or frequently disturbed rooting environment can slow establishment and increase rot risk.
  • Unstable light or humidityLarge swings in conditions can slow a stressed cutting even when the node itself is viable.
What to do next

Change one thing at a time

1Confirm the node

Use the propagation guide to identify the node and growth point before waiting for a leaf-only cutting to produce a vine.

2Stabilize the medium

Keep the node hydrated but oxygenated and avoid changing media repeatedly without a clear reason.

3Wait for established roots

Transition to normal potting mix only after the cutting has developed a stronger root system.

A rooted leaf is not the same as a growing plant

For climbing aroids, the key question is whether the cutting includes stem tissue capable of producing new growth. Confirm that first, then evaluate the rooting environment rather than changing methods repeatedly.
