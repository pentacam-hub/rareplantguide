Root Rot in Rare Houseplants: What to Check Before Repotting

Suspect root rot? Check moisture, root texture, color, smell and plant decline before cutting roots or changing the entire care routine.

Use the Symptom Checker Browse all plant problems
Quick answer

What this symptom can mean

Root rot should be confirmed from the root zone, not diagnosed from yellow leaves alone. The strongest clues are roots that are soft, dark, failing or foul-smelling together with a pot that stays wet and a plant that continues to decline.

Check first

Look for these clues

  1. Check how long the potting mix stays wet after watering.
  2. Inspect accessible roots for firmness, color and damaged tissue.
  3. Smell the root zone if the plant is declining despite wet soil.
  4. Look for several symptoms together rather than relying on one yellow leaf.
Possible causes

Do not assume one cause

  • Water staying around roots too longA dense or degraded mix, oversized pot or watering pattern can leave the root zone wet for too long.
  • Damaged roots after stress or repottingRoot injury can reduce water uptake and make the plant look thirsty even while the mix remains wet.
  • A non-root problemYellowing or drooping can also come from acclimation, light or normal leaf turnover, so inspect roots before assuming rot.
What to do next

Change one thing at a time

1Confirm the root damage

Use texture, smell and visible root condition as evidence before removing tissue.

2Remove only failing material

Follow the detailed root-rot guide for cleaning damaged roots instead of aggressively cutting healthy tissue.

3Rebuild the root environment

Use an airy mix, appropriate pot size and a watering routine based on actual drying.
