Variegation problem

Why Is My Rare Plant Losing Variegation?

Diagnose fading or reduced variegation by comparing genetics, normal leaf variation, light, stress and the active growth point before changing care.

Use the Symptom Checker Browse all plant problems
Quick answer

What this symptom can mean

Reduced variegation is not one diagnosis. Start by checking whether the change is persistent across new growth, whether the stem or growth point also looks greener, and whether the plant has recently experienced a major care change.

Check first

Look for these clues

  1. Compare the newest two or three leaves instead of a single leaf.
  2. Inspect the stem and growth point where future leaves are forming.
  3. Check whether the plant is also stretching, shrinking leaf size or showing other light-related signs.
  4. Note recent repotting, shipping, root trouble or other stress.
Possible causes

Do not assume one cause

  • Genetic or chimeric reversionThe active growth point is producing progressively greener growth and the stem pattern supports that change.
  • Normal pattern variationVariegated plants can produce leaves with different amounts or arrangements of color without a permanent loss.
  • Light or general stressChanges in growing conditions can appear alongside weaker or different-looking new growth, so diagnose the whole plant.
What to do next

Change one thing at a time

1Identify the variegation type

Use the variegation guide to understand whether the pattern is chimeric, genetic or associated with another mechanism.

2Check the growth point

For climbing aroids, use the stem and active node as evidence before making a pruning decision.

3Correct the confirmed problem

If light, roots or reversion is the likely cause, follow the specific guide rather than changing everything at once.
