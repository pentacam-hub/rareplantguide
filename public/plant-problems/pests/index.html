Rare Houseplant Pests: Identify Thrips, Mites, Mealybugs & Scale

See webbing, sticky residue, scarring or visible bugs? Inspect leaves, stems and new growth to narrow the pest before treating a rare houseplant.

Use the Symptom Checker Browse all plant problems
Quick answer

What this symptom can mean

Pest symptoms overlap, so identify the evidence before choosing a treatment. Check both sides of leaves, petioles, stems and new growth for webbing, insects, residue, scale-like bumps or feeding damage.

Check first

Look for these clues

  1. Inspect the undersides of leaves and the newest growth closely.
  2. Look for fine webbing, sticky residue, pale scarring, black specks or visible insects.
  3. Check stems and petioles for attached bumps or cottony material.
  4. Inspect nearby plants before assuming the problem is isolated.
Possible causes

Do not assume one cause

  • Spider mitesFine webbing and stippled or pale leaf damage are important clues to inspect more closely.
  • ThripsScarring, distorted new growth and tiny insects or dark specks can point toward thrips.
  • Mealybugs or scaleCottony clusters or attached scale-like bumps on stems and leaves are easier to confirm visually.
What to do next

Change one thing at a time

1Isolate the plant

Keep a suspected infestation away from the rest of the collection while you confirm what is present.

2Identify before treating

Use the detailed pest guide to match the visible evidence to the likely pest.

3Follow the product label

Use plant-safe control methods according to label directions and repeat inspections until no evidence remains.
