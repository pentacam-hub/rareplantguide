"Losing variegation" can mean a few different things, and collectors often panic before identifying which one they're actually seeing. For general care basics, start with the [Complete Rare Houseplant Care Guide](/posts/rare-houseplant-care-guide/).

## Is It Reversion, or Something Else?

If new leaves are coming in solid green while older leaves stay variegated, that's classic **reversion** — covered in depth in [Why Is My Variegated Plant Turning Green?](/posts/variegated-plant-turning-green/). The short version: it's almost always a light or stress issue, and it's addressed by improving light and pruning back to the last variegated node.

But there are other patterns worth distinguishing:

## Natural Variation Between Leaves (Not a Problem)

Many variegated plants naturally produce leaves with varying amounts of white or color from one leaf to the next — this is normal genetic variability, not decline. A single less-variegated leaf among otherwise healthy, varied growth usually isn't cause for concern.

## Bleaching (Too Much Light, Not Too Little)

Confusingly, the opposite problem can look similar at first glance: white variegated sections **scorching or turning papery/brown** in direct sun. This isn't reversion — it's light damage. Variegated tissue has no chlorophyll to protect it from UV, making it more vulnerable to sunburn than solid green leaves. The fix here is less direct light, not more — see the [grow light guide](/posts/best-grow-lights-rare-houseplants/) for correct placement.

## Virus-Related Variegation Changes

Some variegation (notably certain Monstera patterns) is caused by a virus rather than a genetic mutation. Virus-based variegation can be less stable and may shift or fade differently than mutation-based variegation, largely independent of care. If a plant was sold specifically as "virus variegated," some pattern drift over time is expected and not necessarily fixable through care changes.

## What to Check, in Order

1. Is it new growth that's greener, or old growth changing? (New growth greener = reversion, likely light-related)
2. Is the pattern fading/scorching rather than turning solid green? (Likely light damage, not reversion)
3. Has the plant been stressed recently — repotted, moved, watering changed? (Stress-triggered reversion)

Correctly identifying which of these you're seeing determines the fix — treating light damage like reversion (by adding more light) will make bleaching worse, not better.
