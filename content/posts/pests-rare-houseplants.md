A pest infestation on a rare plant is easiest to control when you identify the pattern early instead of treating every damaged leaf the same way.

**Quick identification:** fine webbing and pale stippling suggest **spider mites**; silver streaks and tiny dark specks point toward **thrips**; white cottony clusters suggest **mealybugs**; and firm brown shell-like bumps are typical of **scale**. Isolate the affected plant before treating it.

| Pest | Fastest clue | Where to check first |
|---|---|---|
| Spider mites | Fine webbing + pale stippling | Leaf undersides and petiole joints |
| Thrips | Silver streaks + dark specks | New growth and leaf surfaces |
| Mealybugs | White cottony clusters | Nodes, joints and crevices |
| Scale | Firm brown shell-like bumps | Stems and leaf undersides |

A stressed plant can also yellow, droop or grow poorly without pests. Look for direct evidence before treating. For general care fundamentals, see the [Complete Rare Houseplant Care Guide](/posts/rare-houseplant-care-guide/).

## Spider Mites

**Signs:** Fine webbing between leaves and stems, tiny yellow/white speckling on leaves, leaves looking dusty or dull.

**Why rare plants are vulnerable:** Spider mites thrive in dry conditions, especially when plants are already stressed.

**Treatment:** Isolate the plant, wipe leaves down with a damp cloth, and treat with an appropriate insecticidal soap or horticultural product according to its label. Reinspect regularly because one treatment may not catch every life stage.

## Mealybugs

**Signs:** Small white, cottony clusters, usually in leaf joints and along stems.

**Treatment:** Remove visible insects carefully and use an appropriate labeled houseplant treatment. Mealybugs hide in crevices, so inspect nodes and repeat checks rather than assuming one pass solved the infestation.

## Scale

**Signs:** Small, brown, shell-like bumps on stems and leaf undersides that do not wipe off easily. They are often mistaken for plant tissue or disease.

**Treatment:** Remove visible scale carefully without damaging the stem, then follow with an appropriate labeled horticultural treatment if needed. Continue checking hidden stem and leaf joints.

## Thrips

**Signs:** Silvery streaks or patches on leaves, tiny dark specks, and distorted new growth.

**Treatment:** Isolate the plant promptly. Sticky traps can help monitor adults, while an appropriate labeled houseplant treatment can target the infestation. Recheck new growth and nearby plants because thrips can move through a collection.

## Fungus Gnats

**Signs:** Small flies hovering around the soil surface. Adults are mainly a nuisance, but persistent wet substrate often supports the problem.

**Treatment:** Let the upper substrate dry appropriately for the plant rather than keeping it continuously wet, improve drainage where needed, and use sticky traps to monitor adults.

## General Prevention

- Quarantine new plants before placing them next to an established collection.
- Inspect leaf undersides, new growth and stem joints regularly.
- Keep tools and propagation containers clean.
- Avoid keeping the root zone unnecessarily wet; see the [Best Soil for Variegated Monstera](/posts/best-soil-mix-variegated-monstera/) for an example of an airy aroid mix.
- If light is weak and the substrate stays wet for a long time, compare your setup with the [Rare Aroid Indoor Light Guide](/posts/how-much-light-do-your-rare-aroids-actually-need-a-real-talk-guide-to-happy-leaves/).

Catching an infestation before it spreads across a collection makes isolation and treatment much easier.
