Propagation is how collectors turn one expensive plant into several — and it's also how you preserve a specific variegation pattern before a stem section reverts to green.

**Quick answer:** take a cutting with at least one node and visible variegation, root it in water or sphagnum moss under bright indirect light, and pot it into a chunky aroid mix once roots reach 3-5cm. Variegated cuttings root slower than solid green ones — patience matters more than technique here.

![Variegated Monstera cutting rooting in a glass jar of water](/images/propagation-water-rooting.jpg)

## Step 1: Choose the Right Cutting

Look for a section of stem with:
- At least **one node** (the small bump where roots and new leaves emerge — a cutting without a node will not root)
- Visible **variegation on the leaf or stem itself**, not just on nearby leaves — cuttings taken from all-green sections will grow all-green
- Healthy, undamaged tissue with no signs of rot or pest damage

👉 If you're not sure why this matters: [Why Is My Variegated Plant Turning Green?](/posts/variegated-plant-turning-green/)

## Step 2: Make the Cut

Using clean, sharp scissors or pruning shears (sterilize with rubbing alcohol to avoid spreading disease), cut just below a node at a slight angle, leaving at least one leaf attached to the cutting if possible.

## Step 3: Choose a Rooting Method

| Method | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Water propagation | Submerge the node, change water every 4-7 days, roots in 3-6 weeks | Watching root progress visually |
| Sphagnum moss | Wrap the node in damp moss, enclose in a clear bag for humidity | Highly variegated cuttings, which often root slower in water |

## Step 4: Provide the Right Conditions

- Bright, indirect light (not direct sun, which stresses a rootless cutting)
- Warmth — 22-27°C speeds up root development
- Patience — variegated cuttings often root slower than solid green ones, since they have less chlorophyll to fuel the process

## Common Mistakes Collectors Make

- **Taking a cutting with no visible node** — it will never root, no matter how healthy the leaf looks
- **Changing water too rarely** — stagnant water promotes rot at the cut site before roots can form
- **Potting too early** — moving a cutting to soil before roots are at
