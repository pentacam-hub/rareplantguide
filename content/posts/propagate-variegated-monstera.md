Propagation is how collectors turn one expensive plant into several — and it's also how you preserve a specific variegation pattern before a stem section reverts to green. For general care fundamentals first, see the [Complete Rare Houseplant Care Guide](/posts/rare-houseplant-care-guide/).

## Step 1: Choose the Right Cutting

Look for a section of stem with:
- At least **one node** (the small bump where roots and new leaves emerge — a cutting without a node will not root)
- Visible **variegation on the leaf or stem itself**, not just on nearby leaves — cuttings taken from all-green sections will grow all-green, a form of reversion covered in [Why Is My Variegated Plant Turning Green?](/posts/variegated-plant-turning-green/)
- Healthy, undamaged tissue with no signs of rot or pest damage

## Step 2: Make the Cut

Using clean, sharp scissors or pruning shears (sterilize with rubbing alcohol to avoid spreading disease), cut just below a node, leaving at least one leaf attached to the cutting if possible.

## Step 3: Choose a Rooting Method

**Water propagation:**
- Place the cutting in a clear container with the node submerged, leaves above water
- Change the water every 4-7 days to prevent bacterial buildup
- Roots typically appear in 3-6 weeks

**Sphagnum moss propagation** (often better for variegated cuttings, which can be slower to root):
- Wrap the node in slightly damp, wrung-out sphagnum moss
- Enclose in a clear bag or container to maintain humidity
- Check weekly and mist if the moss feels dry

## Step 4: Provide the Right Conditions

- Bright, indirect light (not direct sun, which stresses a rootless cutting)
- Warmth — 22-27°C speeds up root development
- Patience — variegated cuttings often root slower than solid green ones, since they have less chlorophyll to fuel the process

## Step 5: Transition to Soil

Once roots reach 3-5 cm long, pot the cutting into the [chunky, well-draining mix](/posts/best-soil-mix-variegated-monstera/) recommended for rare aroids. Keep humidity slightly elevated for the first couple of weeks as the plant adjusts.

## Common Propagation Problems

- **Cutting rots instead of rooting** — usually stagnant water or moss kept too wet; refresh water more often or let moss dry slightly between checks
- **No root growth after 6+ weeks** — check the node is actually submerged/in contact with moss, and increase warmth
- **New growth comes in all green** — the cutting reverted; this is more common in highly variegated cuttings with very little chlorophyll to sustain new growth
